1. Shared request seed across repeats

Effect. The engine draws sampling noise from the request seed. Repeats with one seed draw on the same noise. They still differ, through batching and nondeterminism, but that is not a meaningful sample of how often a question fails.

Control. Screens: a distinct seed per repeat (5000 + repeat). GPQA: pass p sends 1233 + p. tools/verify.py rejects shared seeds.

Status. Controlled. Earlier shared-seed results were withdrawn and replaced (ledger).

Evidence: supported (Fisher p = 0.003)

Same configuration (3.25bpw · tpurtell 0.9.1 · DFlash2 ×3), question 88, 12 repeats, 12 concurrent, fresh server:

The control was added by a dated amendment after the first arms had been seen. Its reading rule (9 or more failures: the fixed seed explains most of the difference) was written before it ran (comparisons/glm53-flash-fixed-seed-control).

2. Answer extraction (flexible-extract)

Effect. lm-eval's raw flexible-extract takes the last parenthesised capital letter in a reply. A reply that names other options or uses chemistry notation after its answer is misread. Net effect: raw scores run 0.5 to 3.5 points low per pass, 1.5 on average.

Control. An audited stated-answer score (correct_stated) is published per row beside the raw score. Raw stays the headline so runs stay comparable.

Status. Measured. The misread is deterministic: rerunning never reveals it.

Detail
  • Over the 16 published passes: 49 correct answers scored wrong, 3 wrong stated answers credited.
  • Causes: labels of other options mentioned after the answer, chemistry notation such as (H) or (R), answers given as a boxed or bold letter.
  • The stated-answer judgement rests on reply text, which is not published; the per-row judgement is (protocols/gpqa-diamond/v1.md).

3. KV saturation

Effect. When running requests fill the KV pool, the rest wait, so fewer run at once than the client sends. 4bpw TR3 (Brandon) leaves a 1,377,179-token pool: 4.17 requests at the token cap.

Control. Its three-pass record runs 4 requests at once, labelled concurrency 4. No request waited.

Status. Controlled for the record. Earlier 4bpw runs at 8 are kept and labelled.

Evidence
  • tpurtell 0.9.1 at 8 concurrent: requests waited for KV in 653 of 1,417 status lines. At 4: none waited (peak usage 91.7%, no preemption).
  • Pass 1 at 8 vs at 4, same configuration: 13 / 13 questions only one got right (p = 1.00), 0.0 points, empty answers 3 / 5 (p = 0.73). No difference detected in one pass.
  • The 4bpw runs on 0.8.0 and 0.9.0 ran at 8 and kept no server log; whether requests waited is not recorded.
  • On tpurtell 0.7.0 + kpool fixes the 4bpw pool was 437,563 tokens; the engine crashed when it filled at 8 concurrent requests (run kept as INVALID).
  • Component screen arm V79: the pool was full for part of the run.

4. Pass-to-pass noise

Effect. One configuration's passes, each with its own seed, differ by 0.5 to 3.5 points. That is as wide as pass 1 across all ten configurations (84.3-87.9%).

Control. Three passes per record. Questions are the unit: a question answered three times counts once.

Status. Measured (descriptive).

What it limits
  • One pass per configuration resolves only differences of about 5 points.
  • Three passes: accuracy differences smaller than about 3 points are neither shown nor excluded.
  • Of 198 questions, 154-160 are right in all three passes of a configuration, 10-12 in none, 28-32 vary.

5. Batching and single-request nondeterminism

Effect. The engine is not bitwise reproducible, even one request at a time. Batching adds more variation at 8 or 12 concurrent requests.

Control. Compare outcomes over many draws and questions, never single transcripts. Repeat a measurement to get its noise floor.

Status. Measured (supported). Why single requests vary is open.

6. Hard questions chosen from seed-1234 data

Effect. Questions 88 and 79 were picked as hard from runs that all sent seed 1234. With distinct seeds, question 88 fails in 1-3 of 12 draws, far less often than those runs suggested.

Control. Screen results are reported per question, never pooled.

Status. Stated limit. Two questions are not a benchmark-wide rate.

7. Several changes between releases at once

Effect. tpurtell 0.7.0 as shipped left fewer GPQA questions unanswered than 0.9.1 (4 vs 16 of 594). But the two differ in several ways at once.

Control. Labels name the release as shipped. The component screen changed single layout parts on two questions.

Status. Open. None of the differences was varied alone in GPQA.

What differs, and what was tested
  • Draft depth: 5 vs 3 tokens.
  • Parallel layout: DCP2 with EP2 experts vs DCP1 with MLA layer ownership.
  • Kernel and engine code; vision on vs off.
  • KV pool: 2,758,919 vs 4,707,515 tokens.
  • The two model revisions share the same weight files. Their chat templates differ (0.7.0 served the checkpoint's own template, 0.9.1 the corrected Z.ai template), but both render all 198 GPQA prompts byte-identically.
  • Component screen, at three draft tokens: no tested layout part moved question 88 on 0.9.1, and the 0.7.0 image failed question 79 as often as 0.9.1 (9 of 12 each). Arms that set MLA ownership tp also turned draft-slot sharing off, so those two are not separated.
  • Draft depth, quantization and sampling were not varied.
  • Since 2026-10-10 the completion investigation has compared the two engines' decode against their own prefill (Phase 1), run a completion pilot and a replication of 0.9.1's default layout against 0.7.0's layout bundle on 0.9.1 (no useful separation), and audited the attention, DCP and indexer source.
  • It found two localized defects: the column-511 drop (9), the only one found that differs between the engines in the direction of the gap, and the tail-ring omission (10), which affects 0.7.0 more. Whether either accounts for any of the gap is open; a GPQA arm with the column-511 correction alone is running.

8. Published scores use other weights and harnesses

Effect. Model-card GPQA scores (92.1 NVIDIA, 90.6 Red Hat) used BF16 or NVFP4 weights and harnesses not fully published.

Control. Shown as context only, never ranked against local runs.

Status. Stated limit. No higher-precision reference was run on this hardware, so these runs cannot separate quantization, harness, scoring and runtime effects.

9. Requests in flight and the top-k route

Effect. For each decode row the sparse-attention indexer's top-k selects 512 compressed-key pools, and this engine keeps 511 of them (columns 0-510) to make room for the newest tokens. No top-k path sorts its output, so which pool is discarded depends on the kernel route. On 0.9.1 the route follows the number of decode rows in the batch: up to 16 rows (1-4 requests in flight at 3 draft tokens) take the fused route, more rows the tiled route. On real inputs the tiled route discarded a pool ranked better than 480 of 512 in 88.1% of live rows, the fused route in 0.5%, and 0.7.0's path on the same bytes in 6.9%. Concurrency therefore changes what the model attends. It is a confound for any comparison of GLM-5.3 engines that use B12x top-k kernels: runs at different concurrency, or engines whose route switches at different batch sizes, differ in this as well.

Control. Concurrency is fixed by each protocol and shown in labels when a run departs from it (3). The completion arm runs at 8, as its comparator did; its frozen analysis approximates route exposure from client-side in-flight counts, which does not measure the in-engine route. The column-511 correction always discards the lowest-scoring selected pool, so the discarded pool no longer depends on the route.

Status. Measured: a localized defect (supported). Its effect on completion is open; a GPQA arm with the correction is running.

10. Kpool tail-ring positions omission

Effect. tpurtell 0.7.0 through 0.9.1 build attention metadata without token positions, which upstream vLLM passes. The kpool tail ring then takes a generic slot mapping: from position 8 every tail write goes to a shared null block, so concurrently decoding requests share one ring, and a pool completed across two steps can be compressed from another request's keys. Through stale ids left by the runner's start-up warm-up, a tail write occasionally lands in another request's blocks. The cross-request share grows with the number of requests in flight on 0.9.1, and is total on 0.7.0 at every concurrency, where the two kpool issues fixed in 0.9.0 add to it.

Control. None in the released engines; every GLM-5.3 record compared here carries it. A fix (positions passed as upstream does, upstream's ownership guard, a write that replayed graphs read) is prepared and verified locally. The completion arm excludes it by design, so the arm and its comparator carry the same defect.

Status. Measured: a localized defect (supported). Its effect on completion or accuracy is untested. 0.7.0 is the more affected engine, so this defect does not explain its better completion.

11. NaN-byte index rows

Effect. Stale tail ids (10) write raw indexer key and gate rows into other requests' indexer pages, where some of the bytes decode as NaN when read as compressed keys. The kernel's scores stay finite (inferred: its max with 0 maps NaN dot products to 0), so such pools can still be selected and attended.

Control. None in the released engines. The gate reported them separately from its selection checks. The column-511 correction does not repair them.

Status. Measured in one gate run; not measurable from GPQA outputs. They limit what a negative completion result for the column-511 correction could exclude.

Evidence
  • Gate run, shipped graph mode, fp64 re-score of sampled rows: at least one selected pool with NaN bytes in 7.9% of decode rows on rank 0 and 32.5% on rank 1 (prefill rows 3.9% and 20.0%). Kernel NaN or infinite scores in serving: 0.
  • The gate attributes them, by inference, to the tail-ring family: mainly on rank 1, persisting in prefix-cached blocks.
  • In stage 2 the same family showed as 16 index-K pools byte-identical across two different requests.
  • Receipts: gate result.

12. Historical control for the completion arm

Effect. The completion arm is compared with the 0.9.1 record made on 2026-10-08 and 10-09. Whatever differs between then and now (server sessions, cache state, timing and batch composition, other load on the second GPU) is confounded with the correction.

Control. A comparator audit before any arm data: the arm's image is built from the release digest with one changed file; weights, prompts, tokenizer, parsers, sampling, seeds, concurrency, admission, warm-up and server settings match; the session split (pass 1, then passes 2-3, each on a fresh server) is mirrored; desktop graphics on the second GPU are logged, not controlled. The analysis, frozen before data, states exchangeability as an assumption.

Status. Stated limit. Under the frozen reading rules a positive result supports improvement relative to the recorded baseline only, not an explanation of the 0.7.0-0.9.1 gap.

Evidence
  • Comparator audit: no material unexplained configuration difference.
  • Frozen analysis, section 5: matching recipes and seeds does not make the comparison a randomized experiment.
  • Dry runs reproduce the published 0.7.0 vs 0.9.1 comparison and give zero effect for 0.9.1 against itself (arm-col511-plan/).

13. Router precision and the routed-expert SwiGLU clamp

Effect. Two departures from upstream vLLM, in every release. On this GPU family the MoE router's fp32-output path is not enabled, so routing logits are computed in bf16 although the checkpoint asks for fp32 routing (upstream enabled it for this family, vllm#54048). The routed experts never receive the checkpoint's swiglu_limit (10); shared experts and dense layers are clamped.

Control. None. Both are the same in all releases, so neither can explain a difference between 0.7.0 and 0.9.1; both can enter any comparison with other runtimes or with published scores (8).

Status. Untested. An earlier clamp comparison patched a path this quant does not run and is not evidence either way (upstream comparison).

14. Graph vs eager execution for instrumentation

Effect. In this build the sparse indexer is not an eager-break op, so a Python probe inside it does not run under CUDA graphs. Measurements taken with such a probe ran in eager mode (stage 2's capture, phase 3 in place), while GPQA runs use the shipped FULL_AND_PIECEWISE graphs. A probe in eager mode adds per-step synchronisation; the top-k route depends only on the row count.

Control. The gate ran the column-511 correction under the shipped graphs with an observe-only device-side checker, and matched its calls to the runner's FULL graph replays on both ranks (no eager fallback). The tail-ring runs used a device-side guard in shipped graph mode. Throughput figures carry each run's own instrumentation and are not clean measures of a fix's cost.

Status. Controlled for the gate and the tail-ring verification. Eager-mode figures (stage 2, phase 3) are labelled as such in the investigation.

15. Long-context decode vs prefill measures continuation after the reply

Effect. decode-prefill-consistency generates with ignore_eos, so a trace continues after the model has ended its reply. In all 24 traces kept by the completion investigation's steps 5-6 the reply had ended at 637-5,377 generated tokens: the 8,000-15,999-token bin is entirely forced continuation (repeated synthetic turns, up to 93 terminators). A long-context difference in that bin measures agreement on text the model would not produce in normal use.

Control. Stated with the measure (protocol v2 note); the long-context bins are not used as evidence about non-completion.

Status. Stated limit. Step 4 kept no traces (the same pattern is likely, not verified). Where the 2,600-token v1 traces cross the end of the reply was not audited (amendment 7).
