The questions

  1. Which weights and engine release to run on two RTX PRO 6000 GPUs: accuracy, completion, speed, KV capacity.
  2. Why some hard GPQA questions do not finish within the 327,680-token budget (investigation).
  3. Which factors besides the configuration move a result, and how to control them (CONFOUNDS.md).
  4. Why tpurtell 0.7.0 left fewer GPQA answers empty than 0.9.1 (investigation).

  • A control arm sent seed 1234 on every repeat, as the earlier screens had: 11 of 12 failed, against 3 of 12 with distinct seeds (Fisher p = 0.003).
  • So repeats that share a seed are not a real sample. Two sets of results were withdrawn (below).

2026-10-10. The GPQA redo.

  • Three passes per configuration, one request seed per pass, for three configurations.
  • One configuration's passes differ by 0.5 to 3.5 points.
  • Documents reorganized around results, with this ledger and the confounds page.
  • New completion investigation, Phase 0 (existing data, no GPU): 0.7.0's higher GPQA score comes entirely from question-passes 0.9.1 left empty. Where both answered (577 of 594), raw accuracy is identical (516 vs 516).
  • Completion investigation, Phase 1 (12 decode-vs-prefill runs, 7.18 GPU-hours): no step found 0.9.1's decode agreeing worse with its prefill than 0.7.0's; at 8,000-15,999 tokens 0.7.0's agreed worse. Below 2,044 tokens disagreement is higher with speculation on, in both engines.

Neither step measured completion.
  • Correction: earlier text said both servers loaded the same vendored chat template. They did not. 0.7.0 served the checkpoint's own template, 0.9.1 the corrected Z.ai template. Both render all 198 GPQA prompts byte-identically, so no number changes.

2026-10-10, evening. What the long-context measure covered; a completion pilot.

  • Trace audit of the 24 traces kept by Phase 1 steps 5-6 (CPU; amendment 7): in every trace the model had closed its reasoning and ended its reply at 637-5,377 generated tokens. The protocol keeps generating past the end (ignore_eos), so the 8,000-15,999-token bin of steps 4-6 is entirely continuation after the reply had ended. That measure says nothing about non-completion. Step 4 kept no traces; the same pattern is likely there, not verified.
  • Completion pilot (amendment 8, manifest frozen before the run): 0.9.1 at its default layout against the 0.9.1 EP2/DCP2 diagnostic bundle (step 6's layout), three target questions and a control, original and fresh seeds, 8 requests in flight, normal stopping, caps of 65,536 or 131,072 tokens. Original seeds: no reply unanswered at the cap in either layout. Fresh seeds: 2 unanswered at the cap with the default layout (q109, q170), 0 with the bundle; one draw each, within chance. 2.01 GPU-hours.

2026-10-11. Replication.

  • Four new fresh seeds each on q109 and q170, same arms and caps (amendment 9): 0 vs 0 unanswered. With the pilot's fresh seeds, 2 of 10 vs 0 of 10 target draws (one-sided p ≈ 0.24): no useful separation. 1.54 GPU-hours; the investigation's total 14.10.

2026-10-11. Source audit, and which pool the 511-pool slice drops.

  • Kernel and DCP source audit (amendments 10-10a; CPU; three independent audits of the 0.7.0, 0.8.0 and 0.9.1 source): no defect unique to 0.9.1 in attention, the DCP combine, record readers, graphs or scratch. The known indexer write defects are 0.7.0's and 0.8.0's and fixed in 0.9.1. Common to every version: the top-k selects 512 compressed-key pools and the engine keeps output columns 0-510 to make room for the newest tokens. No top-k path sorts its output, so the discarded pool (the one in column 511) is not guaranteed to be the weakest. Upstream vLLM keeps all 512.
  • Correction: the looping investigation's code question 6 said these releases' top-k emits winners first, so the discarded pool would be among the lowest-scored. No top-k path sorts its output; a dated note on that question now points here (looping, open questions).
  • Stage 1 (each release's top-k kernels on frozen synthetic inputs; one GPU, no model; 0.10 GPU-hours): on 0.9.1's tiled route (5-8 requests in flight at 3 draft tokens) the discarded pool ranked better than 480 of 512 in 94-98% of rows at 1,024-16,384 pools; on the fused route (1-4 requests) 0-1%; on 0.7.0's path 3-21%.
  • Stage 2 (real serving inputs captured on 0.9.1 in eager mode, replayed through each release's kernels; 1.16 GPU-hours): 88.1% of 9,944 live tiled decode rows; fused 0.5%; 0.7.0's path on the identical bytes 6.9%. The route, which follows the number of requests in flight, alone decides it. Selected sets matched an fp64 reference.
  • Phase 3 (0.58 GPU-hours): switching the two-level fold off did not meet the criterion (the weakest pool in column 511 in 39.8% of tiled rows); moving the lowest-scoring selected pool into column 511 did, in 100% of rows offline and in place, with the selected set unchanged. This is a correction to the engine's 511-pool selection policy, not a restoration of upstream's 512-pool policy.

2026-10-11. The kpool tail ring.

  • In stage 2's second capture, 16 index-K pools were byte-identical across two different requests, some decoding as NaN. A CPU forensic pass found raw indexer key and gate rows for tail-ring slots 4-7, written into blocks then owned by other requests (amendment 10d).
  • Cause (amendment 10e; CPU trace with the real kernels): this build builds attention metadata without token positions, which upstream vLLM has passed since GLM-5.3-Flash support landed. Without them, every tail write from position 8 goes to a shared null block, and through stale ids left by the runner's start-up warm-up, occasionally into another request's blocks. The same omission is in 0.7.0's source. Amendment 10d had nominated a stale request row or a block-table staging race; no staging race was found (25,000 steps against a CPU shadow).
  • Verification (amendment 10f; 1.16 GPU-hours): unmodified 0.9.1 in its shipped graph mode sent 98% of tail writes to the null block on each rank, and 6 into live attention blocks. With positions passed and a graph-safe write: 0 and 0; 104 of 104 requests completed; throughput unchanged.
  • A CPU harness running each release's real kernels: 0.7.0 gets 100% of decode-completed pools wrong at 1, 2 and 8 requests in flight; 0.9.1 0% at 1, 47-57% at 2 and 78-90% at 8. 0.7.0 is the more affected engine, so this defect does not explain its better completion. The investigation's total: 17.11 GPU-hours.

2026-10-11. Upstream comparison, gate, completed tail fix, and the completion arm.

  • Comparison with upstream vLLM (CPU): all three releases carry the same vLLM development commit, not an ancestor of upstream main. No further reachable correctness divergence on the GLM-5.3 paths. Two divergences outside them affect every release and are untested: router logits computed in bf16 on this GPU family (upstream enabled the fp32 path for it, vllm#54048), and no SwiGLU clamp on the routed experts.
  • An independent assessment was adopted (amendment 11): validate the column-511 correction in the shipped graph configuration first, then test completion.
  • Gate (amendments 11-11a; 0.72 GPU-hours): passed all four conditions in one full server run under the shipped CUDA graphs, with an observe-only device checker. It found the prefill branch affected too (rows with exactly 512 candidates came back in index order, so the newest pool was dropped; 2,200 rows); the frozen correction covers it. At least one selected pool with NaN bytes in 7.9% (rank 0) and 32.5% (rank 1) of sampled decode rows: the tail-ring defect, not repaired by this correction.
  • Tail fix completed (amendment 11; 1.15 GPU-hours): positions passed, upstream's ownership guard and the graph-safe write, in two variants (with the sparse-MLA causal-length source unchanged, or switched as upstream does). One shipped-graph run each: 0 null-block, 0 foreign and 0 attention-block writes on both ranks. The investigation's total before the arm: 18.97 GPU-hours.
  • Comparator audit and frozen analysis (amendment 11b, before any arm data): the comparator is the existing clean 0.9.1 three-pass record, a historical control; no material unexplained configuration difference. Primary test: one-sided exact sign-flip on per-question differences in empty replies. Reading: at most 6 empties with p < 0.05 supports a contribution relative to the recorded baseline; 7-12 with p < 0.05 is a modest reduction, not support for explaining the gap; otherwise inconclusive or not supported. Power 0.82-0.87 if the arm matches 0.7.0's per-question rates, 0.95 for a 75% reduction, 0.51 for 50%.
  • Correction (cost): amendment 11 asked for up to 13 GPU-hours for the arm. That estimate left out pass 1's separate server session; the comparator's three passes had occupied 16.59 GPU-hours. The ceiling was raised to 18.5 before launch.
  • Correction (server starts): notes made while preparing the arm described the comparator's passes 2-3 server log as holding more than one server start ("first server block" in the gate result and the frozen analysis). It holds one; the frozen analysis script counts one start in each comparator log (dry runs). Nothing compared changes.
  • Completion arm started in the afternoon: 0.9.1 with the column-511 correction only, three GPQA passes (request seeds 1234-1236, 8 concurrent, 327,680-token cap, shipped graph mode), the comparator's session split mirrored, ceiling 18.5 GPU-hours. It finished in the evening (next entry). Nothing about its outcome is reported until it has finished and been analysed with the frozen script and read by the frozen rules.

2026-10-11, evening. The completion arm's result.

  • Completion arm finished and was analysed with the frozen script, run unchanged (receipts). Every frozen validity check passed: seeds and sampling as specified, no re-sent request, all 594 (question, pass) pairs matching on prompt, doc, target and message hashes and prompt token counts, the same server-argument fingerprint, and the comparator's published rows reproduced.
  • Empty replies 15 of 594 (2.53%) with the correction vs 16 (2.69%) on the comparator; per pass 4/5/6 vs 5/5/6; all 15 at the token cap. One-sided exact sign-flip p = 0.50 over 13 questions that differ; reduction +0.17 points, 95% question-bootstrap interval -1.01 to +1.35. Reading R3: the localized correction stands but is not supported as the dominant completion explanation. Empties moved between questions: 10 question-passes rescued, 9 newly empty.
  • Secondary: raw accuracy 85.0% vs 86.9% (-1.85 points, 95% -5.05 to +1.35), stated 86.7% vs 87.9% (-1.18, -4.04 to +1.52; final after the usual hand read of 12 arm rows); not significant, which does not establish no accuracy cost. Answered completion lengths unchanged (median 3,387 vs 3,508 tokens). 76-84% of busy time had 5 or more requests in flight, so the corrected tiled path was heavily exercised.
  • In short: the column-511 change is a correctness fix that did not move completion behaviour. Limits: a historical control (exchangeability assumed, not randomized); the tail-ring defect, present in both records, limits what this negative result can exclude.
  • 17.11 GPU-hours (ceiling 18.5). The investigation's cumulative total: 36.08 GPU-hours. Nothing further is scheduled.

Withdrawn, and what replaced it

Withdrawal notice (2026-10-09). The earlier hard-question screens (hard-prompt-screen v0 and v1, 2026-09-30 to 10-08) sent request seed 1234 on every repeat. Their failure rates, the layout-bisection statistics and the question-level observations drawn from them are withdrawn. Replaced by the component screen (hard-prompt-screen/v2): one question per run, a distinct request seed per repeat.

GPQA passes that repeated one seed (published before 2026-10-09) are withdrawn. Replaced by the three-pass GPQA records, one request seed per pass (comparisons/glm53-flash-gpqa-records).

Evidence and detail
  • Batching made shared-seed repeats vary, but every repeat drew on the same sampler noise. That variation is not a meaningful sample, and the effective sample behind each rate was far smaller than its request count.
  • Evidence: question 88 failed 3 of 12 times with distinct seeds and 11 of 12 with seed 1234 on every repeat, all else equal. In the eight earlier fixed-seed screens of 3.25bpw DCP1 configurations it had failed 62 of 64 repeats.
  • Withdrawn bisection statistics: as released vs the v0.7.0-layout control, with vs without the tail fix, and the verdicts built on them.
  • Statements built on the shared-seed GPQA passes were rewritten from pass 1 on 2026-10-09 (protocols/gpqa-diamond/v1.md).
  • Finding 2 of the kpool investigation rested on the screens and was withdrawn with them.
  • The withdrawn rows remain in the git history (commit 4fbaad2). The bisection's preregistration is kept unchanged, marked withdrawn.
  • The rule now. Screens: a distinct seed per repeat (5000 + repeat), one question per run, the question as the unit (protocols/hard-prompt-screen/v2.md). GPQA: pass p sends 1233 + p. tools/verify.py rejects repeated seeds in a v2 run unless it is labelled a fixed-seed control, rejects GPQA passes that share a seed, keeps only repeat 1 in v0/v1 runs, and rejects comparisons of v0/v1 runs.
